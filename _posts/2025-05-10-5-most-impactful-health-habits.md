When it comes to health, the internet is full of noise. Detox teas, miracle supplements, 30-day challenges. But if you strip all that away and look at what the research consistently shows, a handful of habits stand out above the rest.

Here are the five that have the strongest evidence — and the biggest impact on long-term health.

---

## 1. Sleep 7–9 Hours Every Night

This is not optional. Sleep is when your body consolidates memory, repairs tissue, regulates hormones, and clears metabolic waste from your brain (through the glymphatic system).

Chronic sleep deprivation — even just getting 6 hours instead of 8 — is associated with increased risk of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, depression, and impaired immune function.

**What to do:** Prioritize a consistent sleep schedule. Same bedtime, same wake time — even on weekends. Keep your room dark and cool (around 18–19°C).

---

## 2. Eat Mostly Whole, Unprocessed Foods

No need to follow a specific diet label. The research across nearly all dietary patterns agrees on one thing: ultra-processed foods are harmful, and whole foods are protective.

Fruits, vegetables, legumes, whole grains, nuts, and seeds provide fiber, micronutrients, and phytocompounds that processed foods simply don't.

**What to do:** A simple rule — if it has more than 5 ingredients and you can't recognize most of them, eat it less often.

---

## 3. Move Your Body Regularly (Especially Resistance Training)

Aerobic exercise is great. But resistance training — lifting weights, using bands, bodyweight exercises — may be the single most powerful longevity intervention available to us.

It preserves muscle mass as we age, improves insulin sensitivity, supports bone density, and even benefits mental health through mechanisms beyond just endorphins.

**What to do:** Aim for 2–3 resistance training sessions per week, plus daily walking of at least 7,000–8,000 steps.

---

## 4. Manage Chronic Stress

Acute stress is normal and even useful. Chronic stress — sustained elevation of cortisol — is a slow-burning threat to nearly every system in your body, from your immune response to your gut microbiome to your cardiovascular health.

**What to do:** The most evidence-backed stress management tools are: regular exercise (already covered), quality sleep (also covered), social connection, time in nature, and mindfulness practices. You don't need to meditate for an hour — even 10 minutes of intentional breathing changes your nervous system state.

---

## 5. Get Regular Health Screenings

Prevention is dramatically more effective than treatment. Many of the leading causes of death — cardiovascular disease, certain cancers, type 2 diabetes — have a long window of opportunity for early intervention.

Knowing your numbers (blood pressure, fasting glucose, lipid panel, vitamin D levels) gives you the information to act before things become serious.

**What to do:** Talk to your doctor about age-appropriate screenings. Don't wait until something feels wrong.

---

## The Bottom Line

None of this is revolutionary. Sleep, eat well, move, manage stress, stay informed about your health. But the gap between knowing and doing is where most people get stuck.

Pick one of these five. Focus on it for 30 days. Then add another.

That's the whole strategy.
